# Website clips rendered by the app, kept in the repository as MP4

Status: accepted — 2026-11-08

## Context

The landing page showed only stills. A clip of the popup opening on ⌥T, and of
Replace Selection swapping a sentence, explains the app faster than a caption.
A screen recording would show whatever was on the recording Mac (other apps, a
real name in the menu bar) and drift from the app as its views change.

## Decision

- `TransFlex --clips <dir>` renders the clips from the app's own SwiftUI views
  (`PopupView`, `ReplaceHUDView`, the `Keycaps` view) on a drawn desktop, one
  frame at a time from a script in `ClipMode.swift`, and writes H.264 MP4 at
  30 fps, 1200×750, with the last frame as a PNG still. It renders `hero` and
  `replace`, each light and dark.
- The sample text comes from `App/SampleText.json`, so the Vietnamese strings
  stay out of Swift sources, which must hold no Vietnamese.
- The MP4s live in `site/public/clips/` (about 1.1 MB for the four) and the
  stills in `site/src/assets/clips/`, committed like the screenshots. No video
  host, no Git LFS.
- The page never autoplays from markup: a script picks the movie for the theme
  and plays it unless the reader asked for reduced motion. A clip below the
  fold loads only once it is on screen. Without JavaScript, or with reduced
  motion, the still shows and no movie is downloaded.

## Consequences

- A change to the popup or the HUD is followed by re-running `--clips` and
  committing the new files; nothing checks that the clips are current.
- Each re-render adds about 1 MB of binary to the history.
- Rendering takes 2–4 minutes per clip on an M-series Mac.
- `site/scripts/check-dist.mjs` fails the build when a clip lacks `muted`,
  `loop`, `playsinline` or its stills, has `autoplay`, loads before it is on
  screen, or names a movie missing from the build.
